How Often Should You Pressure Wash a House in Florida?

July 28, 2026 · By Powerhouse Pressure Washing

It is the question we get more than any other, usually while standing in a driveway looking up at a wall the homeowner has not really looked at in two years. The honest answer is that once a year is right for most homes in Destin and along the Emerald Coast. But that number hides a lot of variation. Two houses on the same street can be on completely different schedules depending on which way they face, what is growing over them, and where the sprinklers point. This guide explains what actually drives exterior growth in our climate, how to read the signs on your own property, and how to tell the difference between a house that needs cleaning and one that just needs rinsing.

Why Gulf Coast Homes Get Dirty Faster

Most of what accumulates on a Florida home is not dirt in any meaningful sense. It is alive. The green film on the lower third of your walls is algae. The gray-black patches on shaded siding are mildew and mold. The dark streaks running down your roof are Gloeocapsa magma, a blue-green algae that feeds on the limestone filler in asphalt shingles. All three need the same three things to thrive: moisture, warmth, and a food source. Northwest Florida provides all of it, generously, for most of the year.

Humidity here averages well above seventy percent, and through the summer it rarely drops low enough or long enough for surfaces to fully dry out. Overnight dew forms on almost every clear night. Temperatures stay in the growth range for organic material for nine or ten months out of twelve. And the pollen, dust and organic debris that settle on a wall provide exactly the nutrient film that algae need to establish. In a dry climate like Arizona, a house exterior gets dusty. Here, it gets colonized.

Add salt to that on the coastal side. Salt aerosol carried in off the Gulf deposits on every surface facing the water, and it does two things. It leaves a mineral film that dulls paint and clouds glass, and it is hygroscopic, meaning it pulls moisture out of the air and holds it against the surface. A salt-loaded wall stays damp longer than a clean one, which accelerates the biological growth on top of it. This is why homes in Crystal Beach, Holiday Isle and along Scenic Gulf Drive often need attention on a shorter cycle than homes two miles inland.

The Four Factors That Set Your Actual Schedule

Shade and tree cover. This is the single biggest variable, and it is not close. A roof plane or wall that stays wet until ten in the morning will grow algae several times faster than one that dries by eight. Homes in Bluewater Bay, Kelly Plantation and the older canopied parts of Niceville and Destin frequently need a wash every eight to ten months, while an exposed home on an open lot might comfortably go eighteen. If you have live oaks overhanging the roof, assume you are on the short end.

Distance from the water. Direct salt exposure shortens the cycle. Homes within a few blocks of the Gulf accumulate mineral film continuously, and while it does not look as dramatic as green algae, it dulls the finish and holds moisture. Bay-side and sound-side properties get less salt but often more persistent damp from morning fog.

Irrigation. This one surprises people. Sprinkler heads that throw water onto walls do real damage over time, and not just from the water. Well water in this region carries iron, which oxidizes on contact with air and leaves the orange staining you see at the base of walls and along driveway edges. It also keeps the lower courses of siding permanently damp, which is why the bottom three feet of a wall are almost always the dirtiest part. Turning a single head fifteen degrees can extend the life of a house wash by months.

Siding material. Smooth vinyl sheds growth better than textured stucco. Stucco and rough-finish surfaces have far more surface area and far more places for organic material to lodge, so they hold growth longer and need more dwell time to clean properly. Painted wood sits somewhere in between and is the most vulnerable to being damaged by an aggressive cleaning.

How to Read Your Own House

You do not need a professional to tell you whether your house needs washing. Walk the perimeter and look at four things.

Start with the north side. Whatever is happening on your house is happening there first. If the north elevation shows a green tint at the base of the wall, or a gray cast on the soffits, growth is established and the rest of the house is behind it by a few months. That is your early warning system, and checking it twice a year takes ten minutes.

Do the wipe test. Run a finger firmly down a section of siding you think is clean. If it leaves a visible clean stripe, you have a film. That film is what dulls the color of a house and makes people think their paint is failing when it is not. A house wash frequently returns siding to a color the owner had forgotten it was.

Look up at the roof from across the street. Streaking is much easier to see from a distance and at an angle than from directly below. Faint darkening on the north-facing planes means the algae is established but early, which is the ideal time to treat it. Heavy black streaks running the full slope mean it has been working on the shingle for years.

Check the concrete edges. Driveways and walkways darken so gradually that homeowners genuinely stop seeing it. The trick is to look at the edge where the concrete meets a garage door threshold or a foundation line, somewhere that stayed protected. The color difference between the protected strip and the open slab is the amount of buildup you have.

If two or more of those tests come back positive, you are due. And a note on timing: the best window in this climate is spring, after pollen season has finished but before the summer humidity really settles in. A wash in April or May buys you the cleanest look through the season when you are actually outside using the property.

The Bottom Line

Once a year is the right default for most homes in Destin and the surrounding Emerald Coast. Shorten it to eight or ten months if you are heavily shaded, close to the water, or fighting irrigation overspray, and you can stretch it toward eighteen if you are on an open lot with good drying exposure. The more useful habit is simply checking the north side of your house twice a year, because catching growth early means a routine soft wash rather than a restoration job. If you are not sure where your property falls, we will come look at it and tell you honestly, even if the answer is that you can wait another season.
